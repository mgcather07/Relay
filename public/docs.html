Relay documentation

Everything you need to run your service desk on Relay — from first sign-up to day-to-day queue work, administration, and the security model underneath.

What is Relay?

Relay is help desk software for internal IT teams. It turns phone calls, emails, walk-ups, and self-service requests into one prioritized queue with a live SLA countdown on every ticket, a clean portal for the people asking for help, and on-call rotations built from your actual team roster.

Relay runs entirely in the browser. There is nothing to install on any machine — every computer on your network (or off it) gets the same live workspace, synced in real time.

One queue

Every channel lands in a single list, sorted by how close each ticket is to breaching its target.

Live SLA clocks

Targets count down in real time. At-risk work turns orange an hour out; breaches turn red.

Roles built in

Admins run the workspace, agents work the desk, requesters get a portal that shows only their own requests.

Walled-off data

Each company's workspace is isolated server-side. No other customer can ever read your tickets.

Set up your company Admin

Getting a company onto Relay takes about two minutes:

  1. Create your account. Click Get started on the home page and sign up with your work email and a password (6+ characters).
  2. Name your workspace. On the next screen choose Create a workspace and enter your company name. You become its first administrator.
  3. Look around. Relay seeds three clearly-labeled sample tickets so the desk isn't empty. Remove them any time in Settings → Organization → Remove sample data.
  4. Invite your team. Open Settings → Team & invites. You'll find two codes:
    • AGT-XXXXXXXX — for people who will work tickets (agents)
    • REQ-XXXXXXXX — for everyone else in the company (requesters)
    Share each code with the right group — chat, email, or a sticky note on the IT room door all work.
  5. Tune the basics. In Settings, set your time zone and business hours, and adjust the channels and categories agents pick from when logging tickets.
💡Tip: promote a second admin early (Settings → Team & invites → change a member's role) so workspace administration never depends on one person.

For agents Agent

  1. Sign up on the Relay home page with your work email.
  2. Choose I have an invite code and enter the AGT-… code from your administrator.
  3. You land on the Desk — the live queue. Start with the Unassigned view and pick something up, or check Breaching soon for anything about to miss its target.

Your day-to-day loop: open a ticket → reply to the requester (or leave an internal note for the next tech) → resolve when it's fixed. Everything you do updates for the whole team in real time, and your open-ticket load shows up in assignment suggestions.

For employees Requester

  1. Sign up with your work email, choose I have an invite code, and enter the REQ-… code (ask IT if you don't have it).
  2. You land in the Help Center — your portal. Click New request, describe the problem in plain words, and say how much it's blocking you. Relay sets the priority and routes it to the right team automatically.
  3. Track every request you've made with live status; reply on the request thread whenever the technician needs something from you.
ℹ️Requesters only ever see their own requests — never the rest of the company's queue.

Workspaces & roles

A workspace is one company's private Relay: its people, tickets, settings, and audit trail. Every account belongs to exactly one workspace, joined either by founding it or by invite code.

RoleSeesCan do
AdminDesk + Portal + Settings Everything an agent can, plus: edit organization settings, manage members and roles, view and regenerate invite codes, manage channels/categories, configure logging, delete tickets and sample data.
AgentDesk + Portal Work the full queue: create, assign, reply, add internal notes, resolve/reopen, bulk actions, dashboard, and on-call pages.
RequesterPortal only Submit requests, track their own tickets, and reply on their own threads. Nothing else is visible or reachable.

Roles are enforced on the server, not just hidden in the interface — see Security & data.

Tickets

A ticket is one request for help, with a conversation thread and an SLA clock. Tickets get sequential IDs per workspace (RLY-1000, RLY-1001, …) allocated atomically, so two agents logging at once can never collide.

How tickets are born

  • Portal: an employee submits a request. Relay maps their impact answer to a priority (see below) and routes it to the desk unassigned.
  • Desk: an agent presses C (or New ticket) to log a call or walk-up on behalf of someone, choosing channel, category, priority, and an assignee — or leaving it in the pool.

Statuses

StatusMeaning
NewNobody has picked it up yet.
TriageBeing classified/routed before real work starts.
In progressAssigned and actively worked. Assigning a ticket sets this automatically, as does a first public reply.
Waiting on userThe desk needs something from the requester.
ResolvedDone. Resolved tickets leave the open views but stay searchable; reopening puts them back in progress.

Replies vs. internal notes

The composer on every ticket has two modes. Public reply goes to the requester and appears on their portal thread. Internal note (highlighted yellow) is visible only to the desk — perfect for handoffs between shifts. The requester never sees internal notes.

Linking and merging

Use Link on a ticket (or Merge from the bulk bar) to connect duplicates and related incidents — for example five reports of the same outage.

Priorities & SLAs

Every priority carries a resolution target. The clock starts when the ticket is created and counts down live on the queue, the detail view, and the dashboard.

PriorityMeansResolution targetFirst-reply guide
P1 · CriticalWhole team or business function down4 hours15 minutes
P2 · HighSomeone is blocked from working8 hours30 minutes
P3 · NormalAnnoying but there's a workaround24 hours4 hours
P4 · LowRequests, nice-to-haves48 hours1 day

Portal requests map impact to priority automatically: “Whole team” → P1, “Blocking my work” → P2, “Annoying” → P3.

Color language everywhere: green = on track, orange = at risk (less than an hour left), red = breach imminent or missed. The Breaching soon view collects everything inside the final hour.

The Desk

The Desk is where agents live: a sidebar of views, the queue, and the ticket detail.

Breaching soonAnything within an hour of missing its target (or already past it).

Filter any view by priority, category, channel, or team using the chips above the queue and the team list in the sidebar. Sort by SLA urgency, priority, or newest.

Working the queue

  • Assign from the assignee pill on any row (or inside a ticket). The picker sorts teammates by current open load and suggests the lightest.
  • Bulk actions: select rows with the checkboxes and a floating bar appears — assign, raise to P1, resolve, or merge in one motion.
  • Macros: one-click reply starters (“Ask for a screenshot”, “Escalating now”, “Fixed — confirm?”) sit under the composer.
  • Knowledge suggestions: when the workspace has knowledge-base articles, likely matches appear beside the ticket with one-click insert into reply.

Command palette

Press ⌘K (or /) anywhere on the desk to search tickets and jump to any view or action — “assign to me”, “resolve”, “who is on call”, straight from the keyboard.

The Portal

The portal (your company Help Center) is what requesters see — and agents can preview it any time with the surface switcher in the top bar.

  • New request: a short form — summary, category, how blocking it is, details. The routing hint under the button shows exactly where it will go and what target it gets.
  • My requests: every request the person has made, with live status and time-to-target.
  • Request thread: a timeline of what's happened — received, picked up, public replies — plus a reply box that goes straight to the technician. Internal desk notes never appear here.

Dashboard

SLA & queue health (sidebar → SLA dashboard) is computed live from your real tickets:

  • Open tickets / Past SLA / Resolved / Unassigned counters.
  • Resolved vs. breached — the last seven days of closes, flagged red when a ticket closed after its target.
  • At risk in the next hour — click through to any ticket about to breach.
  • Agent load — open tickets per person, so rebalancing is a glance, not a meeting.

On-call rotations

Relay builds weekly on-call rotations from your team roster — one track per team, rotating through its members week by week (weeks roll on Monday).

  • This week: cards per track showing the primary, the escalation ladder, and contact details, with one-click New ticket pre-assigned to whoever is on call.
  • Next 8 weeks: the schedule ahead, so people can plan swaps around holidays.
  • Holidays: the observed-holiday calendar with who covers each one.
  • Escalation: if a page isn't acknowledged, escalate up the ladder; the duty manager (your first admin, unless configured otherwise) is always the final rung.
ℹ️Rotations are generated from team membership in Settings — add or move people between teams and the tracks update automatically.

Invites & team Admin

Invite codes

Each workspace has exactly two live codes — one per joining role:

  • AGT-XXXXXXXX joins new members as agents.
  • REQ-XXXXXXXX joins new members as requesters.

Codes are unguessable random tokens. Regenerate issues a fresh code and kills the old one immediately — do this if a code leaks outside the company. Existing members are never affected by regeneration.

Managing people

  • Change a role with the dropdown next to any member (e.g. promote an agent to admin, or move a requester to the desk). It takes effect on their next page load.
  • You can't change your own role — another admin must, which prevents a workspace from accidentally losing its last administrator.

Logging & audit Admin

  • Audit trail: when action logging is on, every create, assign, reply, resolve, role change, and invite regeneration is recorded — who, what, when. The trail is append-only: nothing in it can be edited or deleted, not even by admins.
  • Export: one click exports the trail to CSV.
  • Scorecards: per-agent closed volume and SLA-met percentage, computed from the actual tickets in your workspace.
  • Verbatim logging (storing full reply text in the audit log) is off by default — check with HR before enabling.

Security & data

Relay is multi-tenant with server-side isolation. The rules below are enforced by the database itself (Cloud Firestore security rules), not by the app's interface:

  • Only members of a workspace can read anything in it. There is no cross-workspace query, listing, or lookup — one company can never see another's existence, let alone its tickets.
  • Requesters can only read tickets where they are the requester, and can only append to their own threads.
  • Settings and membership writes require the admin role; the audit trail accepts appends only.
  • Invite codes work like capability tokens: knowing a live code is the only way in, codes can't be listed or enumerated, and regeneration revokes instantly.

What Relay stores

  • Account: name, work email, and a salted password hash (handled by Firebase Authentication — Relay never sees or stores plaintext passwords).
  • Workspace data: tickets and their threads, workspace settings, membership and roles, and the audit trail.

Data lives in Google Cloud (Firestore) with encryption at rest and TLS in transit. Password resets go through the standard email reset flow — Relay staff cannot read or set anyone's password.

Deployment Operators

This section is for whoever operates a Relay deployment (if you're using the hosted service, you can skip it).

Relay ships as a static web app on Firebase Hosting backed by Firebase Authentication and Cloud Firestore. A fresh deployment activates in three steps:

  1. In the Firebase console for the project: Firestore → Create database (production mode) and Authentication → enable Email/Password.
  2. From the repository: bash scripts/setup.sh — registers the web app, writes src/firebase-config.json, builds, and deploys hosting plus the Firestore security rules.
  3. Visit the hosting URL and create the first workspace.

Local development:

npm install
npm run dev              # http://localhost:5173
npx tsc --noEmit         # typecheck
firebase emulators:start --only auth,firestore   # needs Java 21+
# then open http://localhost:5173/?emu=1 to run live mode against emulators

Until a deployment is activated, the site still serves the marketing page and the interactive demo; sign-up explains that accounts aren't enabled yet.

FAQ

Does anything need to be installed on employees' computers?

No. Relay is a website. Any modern browser on any operating system works — office desktops, laptops at home, phones.

Can I be in two workspaces at once?

Not today — an account belongs to one workspace. Use a second email address if you truly need two (for example, a consultant serving two companies).

What happens if an invite code leaks?

Regenerate it (Settings → Team & invites). The old code stops working immediately. People who already joined are unaffected — remove any unwanted member from the same screen.

Can requesters see other people's tickets?

No. The server only lets a requester read tickets they filed themselves. Even a modified client can't fetch anyone else's data.

Can we change the SLA targets?

The four priority targets (4h/8h/24h/48h) are fixed in the current release. A per-plan SLA policy editor is on the roadmap; Enterprise agreements can include custom targets.

Do you send email notifications?

Not yet — updates are live in the app in real time. Email notifications on ticket events are on the roadmap.

Is there an API?

Not yet. If an integration matters to you, tell us which one — sales@relay routes to the team.

How do I delete a ticket?

Admins can delete tickets (agents resolve instead, which preserves history). The three sample tickets can be removed in one click from Settings → Organization.

What browsers are supported?

Current versions of Chrome, Edge, Safari, and Firefox.

Troubleshooting

SymptomLikely cause & fix
“That invite code isn't valid”The code was mistyped or has been regenerated. Codes are exactly AGT-/REQ- plus 8 characters, no spaces — ask your admin for the current one.
“Email and password don't match”Use Forgot password? on the sign-in card to get a reset link, or make sure you're using the email you signed up with.
“This deployment isn't activated yet”The operator hasn't finished activation. The demo still works; accounts will open once Firestore and email sign-in are enabled.
I only see the portal, not the queueYour account joined with a requester code. An admin can change your role to agent in Settings → Team & invites.
Settings is missing from my sidebarSettings is admin-only. Ask an existing admin to promote you.
Changes aren't appearing for a teammateRelay syncs in real time; if it stops, check the network connection and reload the page. Offline edits reconcile automatically when the connection returns.
A ticket shows a negative time like −2hThat's a breach — the ticket is past its resolution target by that amount. It also appears in Breaching soon.
